Key-Value Caching

In standard attention, for an input sequence with \( n \) tokens,

$$ x = [x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n] $$

we compute query, key, and value projections. The query, key and value projections for each token \( x_i \) can be represented as \( q_i \), \( k_i \), \( v_i \) respectively, where:

$$ q_i, k_i, v_i \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times d} $$

Here, \( d \) is the model dimension.

For the full sequence:

$$ Q = \langle q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n \rangle, \quad K = \langle k_1, k_2, \dots, k_n \rangle, \quad V = \langle v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n \rangle $$

where \( \langle \cdot \rangle \) denotes concatenation. Below is an example:

Attention Computation

To compute attention scores, \( S \), we do:

$$ QK^\top = \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \\ q_2 \\ \vdots \\ q_n \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} k_1^\top & k_2^\top & \cdots & k_n^\top \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \cdot k_1 & q_1 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_1 \cdot k_n \\ q_2 \cdot k_1 & q_2 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_2 \cdot k_n \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ q_n \cdot k_1 & q_n \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_n \cdot k_n \end{bmatrix} $$

According to the original attention paper, we scale the dot products by \( \sqrt{d} \).
Thus, $$ S = \frac{Q \cdot K^\top}{\sqrt{d}} $$ But for simplicity, we will omit the scaling factor here since it does not affect the caching argument in this explanation and assume \( S = Q \cdot K^\top\)

  • Time Complexity: \( O(d \cdot n^2) \)
  • Space Complexity: \( O(n^2) \)

We then apply causal masking and softmax to generate normalized scores(aka activation weights), \( A \):

$$ \text{\( S \) + mask} = S_{\text{masked}} \;\xrightarrow{\;\boldsymbol{\text{row-wise softmax}}\;} A $$

The raw attention score matrix \( S \in \mathbb{R}^{5 \times 5} \), for our example is:

Autoregressive Generation

Since this is an autoregressive task, the model predicts the next token. Given an input sequence of \( n \) tokens, we generate the \( (n+1)^{th} \) token:

$$ x_{n+1} = M(x) = M([x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n]) $$

To keep generating further tokens, we feed the output back into the model:

$$ x_{n+2} = M([x_1, x_2, \dots, x_n, x_{n+1}]) $$

and so on.

Recomputing Projections

To generate the \( (n+2)^{th} \) token, we again compute query, key, and value projections:

$$ Q' = \langle q_1, q_2, \dots, q_n, q_{n+1} \rangle $$

$$ K' = \langle k_1, k_2, \dots, k_n, k_{n+1} \rangle $$

$$ V' = \langle v_1, v_2, \dots, v_n, v_{n+1} \rangle $$

We then recompute self-attention using these projections. First, we compute attention scores:

$$ S' = Q'K'^\top = \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \\ q_2 \\ \vdots \\ q_n \\ q_{n+1} \end{bmatrix} \begin{bmatrix} k_1^\top & k_2^\top & \cdots & k_n^\top & k_{n+1}^\top \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \cdot k_1 & q_1 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_1 \cdot k_n & q_1 \cdot k_{n+1} \\ q_2 \cdot k_1 & q_2 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_2 \cdot k_n & q_2 \cdot k_{n+1} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots & \vdots \\ q_n \cdot k_1 & q_n \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_n \cdot k_n & q_n \cdot k_{n+1} \\ q_{n+1} \cdot k_1 & q_{n+1} \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_{n+1} \cdot k_n & q_{n+1} \cdot k_{n+1} \end{bmatrix} $$

But in doing so, most of the computations are repeated. Only the computations involving the new token differ.

Identifying Overlapping Computation

Let's assume a cache (for simplicity) as a Python-like structure:

cache = {}

If you look closely at the attention score matrix:

  • Only the last row and last column are new
  • The remaining \( n \times n \) block is exactly the same as before

$$ \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \cdot k_1 & q_1 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_1 \cdot k_n \\ q_2 \cdot k_1 & q_2 \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_2 \cdot k_n \\ \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ q_n \cdot k_1 & q_n \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_n \cdot k_n \end{bmatrix} \quad \quad \begin{bmatrix} q_1 \cdot k_{n+1} \\ q_2 \cdot k_{n+1} \\ \vdots \\ q_n \cdot k_{n+1} \end{bmatrix} $$ $$ \begin{bmatrix} q_{n+1} \cdot k_1 & q_{n+1} \cdot k_2 & \cdots & q_{n+1} \cdot k_n & q_{n+1} \cdot k_{n+1} \end{bmatrix} $$

The last row, \( R \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times (n+1)} \) is computed as:

$$ R = q \cdot K'^\top $$

The last column (except the last overlapping element), \( C \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times 1} \) is computed as:

$$ C = Q \cdot k_{n+1}^\top $$

$$ S' = $$
s₁₁ s₁₂ s₁₃ s₁₄ s₁₅ s₁₆
s₂₁ s₂₂ s₂₃ s₂₄ s₂₅ s₂₆
s₃₁ s₃₂ s₃₃ s₃₄ s₃₅ s₃₆
s₄₁ s₄₂ s₄₃ s₄₄ s₄₅ s₄₆
s₅₁ s₅₂ s₅₃ s₅₄ s₅₅ s₅₆
s₆₁ s₆₂ s₆₃ s₆₄ s₆₅ s₆₆

To compute the new attention score matrix, we might consider storing:

  • Old attention scores ( \(Q \cdot K^\top\) ) — the overlapping \( n \times n \) block, \( S \)
  • Key projections — needed to compute the last row, \( R \)
  • Query projections — needed to compute the last column, \( C \)

So the cache looks like:


            cache = {
                "attention_scores": S,
                "query_proj": Q,
                "key_proj": K,
            }

Effect of Causal Masking & Softmax

When we apply causal masking, we ignore the upper triangle (above the main diagonal). After applying the causal mask, we ultimately ignore the last column.

So even if the last column \( C \) is not computed, it is totally fine. This means caching query projections is not required at all. The cache now becomes:

cache = {
                "attention_scores": S,
                "key_proj": K,
            }

Incorporating Value Projections

Next, to compute the final attention output, we multiply the normalized attention scores with the value projection:

$$ \text{O'} = \text{\( A' \)} \cdot V' $$

$$ \text{\( A' \)} $$
A₁₁ 0 0 0 0 0
A₂₁ A₂₂ 0 0 0 0

$$ O' $$
O'₁₁ O'₁₂ O'₁₃

O'₆₁ O'₆₂ O'₆₃

Because of causal masking(zero column vector):

  • The first \( n \) rows of the final attention output remain the same.
  • The only new computation is the last row.

The \( (n+1)^{th} \) row is computed as:

$$ \text{\( A' \)}_{\text{n+1}} \cdot V' $$

This shows that value projections are also important and must be cached.

So now the cache becomes:


            cache = {
              "attention_scores": S,
              "key_proj": K,
              "value_proj": V,
            }

Final Observation

One key detail here:

To generate the \( (n+2)^{th} \) token, we do not need the first \( n \) rows of the final attention output. All the information required — the attention of all previous tokens on the \( (n+1)^{th} \) token — is embedded in the last row.

This means we do not need to cache attention scores either.

Final Cache: KV Cache

So finally, the cache contains only:

cache = [
              key_proj,
              value_proj
            ]

And that is Key–Value (KV) Caching.
